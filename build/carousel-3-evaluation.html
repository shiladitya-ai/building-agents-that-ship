The quiet failure

A confident report that was simply wrong.

The agent assessed the tenant, wrote a clean readiness summary, and marked it green. Half the accounts were never actually verified.

Nothing crashed. No stack trace. Just a fluent, plausible judgment that nobody caught — until it reached the customer.

The reframe

A broken action crashes.
A broken judgment lies fluently.

Reliability engineering catches the first. It does nothing for the second.

The moment an agent decides rather than just executes, correctness stops being a runtime property — it becomes an evaluation problem.

Production-Grade Agentic AI@shilsr · 3 / 8
The trap

Fluency reads as correctness.

Polished output disarms reviewers. A well-formatted, confident answer gets rubber-stamped — the better the prose, the less it’s scrutinized.

Your evaluation has to reward being right, not sounding right. Those are different signals, and only one of them ships safely.

Fix 1

Score the trajectory, not just the destination.

Two agents reach “ready.” One verified every account; one guessed. Same answer — very different systems.

Plan→ Tool calls→ Evidence→ Answer

Grade the path: did it check what it claimed to check? Outcome-only metrics can’t tell luck from judgment.

Production-Grade Agentic AI@shilsr · 5 / 8
Fix 2 — two layers

Offline gate. Online guardrail.

Offline gate

  • Golden set of hard cases
  • Runs in CI, blocks release
  • k-run consistency check

Online guardrail

  • Canary on live traffic
  • Watch escalation rate
  • Auto-rollback on drift

One proves it’s good before ship. One proves it’s still good after.

On LLM-as-judge

Useful tool. Not an oracle.

  • Calibrate it against human labels before you trust a single score.
  • Distill its agreement into cheaper, faster checks where you can.
  • Backstop with hard rules for anything safety- or money-critical.

A judge that shares the generator’s blind spots will confidently co-sign the same mistake.
